### Q2 — harmonising fg with restyled bg

- F9. MKL-Harmonizer ("Lightweight Optimal-Transport Harmonization on Edge Devices"): a compact encoder (EfficientNet-B0 modified for 4 channels = RGB + mask) predicts only 12 parameters of a Monge-Kantorovich Linear (optimal-transport) colour filter, applied to the object. On Google Pixel 4a / Pixel 7 with ARCore it runs at 12–15 fps in the current implementation (authors: zero-copy could reach 24–30 fps). Human evaluation on real AR images: perceptually on par with leading baselines; on iHarmony4 (256x256) its MSE is 65.0 vs PCT-Net 29.1, Harmonizer 40.1, INR 67.2 (i.e., worse on the metric, comparable to humans). Stated limitations: too-dark objects often give implausible results; not designed for video. Parameter count not stated. | https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12785 | arXiv | 2025-11 (from arXiv id) | H (fetched; numbers via a summarising fetch tool) | perf, capability, failure | YES for the fps claim (single paper, Android, not iOS)
- F10. My inference from F9 (unverified): a 12-parameter MKL map is a per-region affine colour transform (3x3 + 3 offsets), which Core Image can apply with built-in matrix filters; so the "learned" part reduces to producing 12 numbers, and a closed-form statistics version (Reinhard/MKL from mean and covariance of fg vs bg) needs no model at all. Not verified in any source retrieved.
- F11. PCT-Net (CVPR 2023, Rakuten): a parameter network on a downsampled image predicts pixel-wise affine colour transforms that are applied to the full-resolution image; the repo says CPU is enough for inference; reports foreground-MSE/MSE down >20% and +1.4 dB PSNR on iHarmony4 full-res. No mobile latency or Core ML conversion found. Licence not checked. | https://github.com/rakutentech/PCT-Net-Image-Harmonization | GitHub / IEEE | 2023 | M | capability | YES
- F12. F9 also reports that dense-prediction harmonisers "sometimes produce corrupted results at high resolution", while filter-based (colour-transform) methods avoid this — consistent with PCT-Net's design (F11) and relevant to 12 MP+ exports. | https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12785 | arXiv | 2025-11 | M | failure | YES
- F13. IC-Light (lllyasviel) is a Stable-Diffusion-1.5-based relighting model, released as a text-conditioned and a background-conditioned variant, both taking the foreground as input; it inpaints background and relights globally in one diffusion pass. No Core ML / on-device port found. Consequence: relighting = another SD1.5-class pass on top of the restyle, i.e. T2 cost, not a light add-on. | https://github.com/lllyasviel/IC-Light ; https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14549v1 (DreamLight, describes IC-Light) | GitHub / arXiv | n/d ; 2025-06 | M | capability, landscape | YES (on-device absence is absence of evidence)
- F14. Edge feathering, alpha matting refinement, contact shadows and light wrap: no source retrieved. They are compositing primitives (blur, mask offset, blend); feasibility in Core Image is my engineering belief, unverified. See F2/F17 for the low-res-mask problem: Apple's own mask output is 384x512 (F3), so a 12 MP+ composite must upsample or refine the mask.

### Q3 — upscaling

- F15. Real-ESRGAN Core ML ports tile the image into 512x512 tiles (512 + 10 px pre-pad = 522x522 model input) with overlap blending so one fixed-shape model handles any size; the memory blow-up comes from the full-resolution output tensor, not the model; claim of "under 1 GB" for 64/128-px tiles comes from an unattributed aggregator. The repo targets Apple Silicon Macs (M-series), not iPhone; no iPhone latency for a 12 MP to 48 MP upscale was found. | https://github.com/gil/real-esrgan-coreml (snippet) ; aggregator: https://localaimaster.com/blog/ai-image-upscaling-local | GitHub / blog | n/d ; 2026 | L | memory, perf | YES
- F16. PiperSR (ModelPiper): an ANE-native 2x SR model; vendor reports 22.5 ms ANE prediction for a 640x360 frame, and that feeding the whole frame as one tensor instead of 66 tiles of 128x128 removed ~96% of ANE scheduling overhead; also claims ~7x faster than SPAN and ~160x faster than Real-ESRGAN "on the same hardware family" (device not stated in the snippet). Marketing-register vendor claims; the useful design point is "ANE dislikes many small tiles". | https://github.com/ModelPiper/PiperSR ; https://modelpiper.com/blog/pipersr-44fps-video-upscale-apple-neural-engine | vendor GitHub/blog | n/d | L | perf | YES

A developer-forum thread reports 144x144 videos failing although the API reports a 96x96 minimum. Whether it accepts a still 12 MP frame, its max input size, and iOS 27 availability were NOT retrieved. | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/videotoolbox/vtlowlatencysuperresolutionscalerconfiguration?language=objc ; https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/814646 | Apple Developer docs / forums (snippets) | n/d | L | capability | YES
- F18. Memory arithmetic (computation, not sourced): 4032x3024 (12.2 MP) RGBA8 = 48.8 MB per buffer, RGBA half-float = 97.5 MB; 8064x6048 (48.8 MP) RGBA8 = 195 MB, half-float = 390 MB. Any pipeline holding several full-res buffers at 48 MP is in the 0.5–1 GB range before models; so the "keep original subject at full res, upsample only the restyled background" pattern (design inference, unverified) matters mostly for avoiding an SR pass over the subject.

### Q4 — colour management / HDR / formats (source: WWDC24 10177, fetched)

- F19. Adaptive HDR is stored in HEIF, JPEG and ProRAW as a backward-compatible SDR baseline + metadata + gain map; standard ISO 21496-1 (described as Committee Draft in the 2024 talk — status now not checked); a HEIF has one image (`CGImageSourceGetCount` = 1) whose HDR look is a recipe of baseline + gain map; the gain map is typically half the baseline size, so edits must account for the scale. | https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10177/ | Apple (WWDC24) | 2024-06 | H | capability | no (primary), but stale
- F20. Loading: `CIImage(contentsOf:options:[.expandToHDR: true])` (ImageIO: `kCGImageSourceDecodeToHDR`); gain map as separate image via `.auxiliaryHDRGainMap`; `CIImage.contentHeadroom` is 1.0 for SDR, >1 up to 8 for iPhone HDR photos, 0 when unknown. Default decode is SDR unless expandToHDR is requested. | same | H | capability | no
- F21. Headroom survives scale, crop, warp and certain convolutions; for other operations Core Image does not know the effect and reports headroom 0 (unknown). Built-in filters advertise HDR support in their attribute categories; some photo blend modes do not support HDR (per fetch summary of the talk). Implication: a mask/blend composition graph can silently return unknown headroom. | same | M-H | failure, capability | no
- F22. Editing strategies: (a) HDR-only: after an HDR edit the original gain map can no longer tone-map down to SDR; save as 10-bit HEIF with a PQ colour space (`heifRepresentation(using: .heif10)`); (b) Adaptive HDR (SDR + HDR): `CIContext.writeHEIFRepresentation(of: sdrImage, colorSpace: displayP3, options: [.HDRImage: hdrImage])` — Core Image computes the gain map from the two edits; (c) SDR + original gain map via `.HDRGainMapImage`, valid only for simple edits (crop/rotate/warp); (d) ImageIO route with `kCGImageAuxiliaryDataTypeISOGainMap`. Display P3 is the colour space named for Adaptive HDR SDR baselines; tone-map for display with `CIToneMapHeadroom`; headroom depends on display, brightness, battery. | same | H | capability | no
- F23. Implication for this pipeline (my inference, unverified): a diffusion background is 8-bit SDR with no highlight information, so a recomposed image is SDR-look at best; keeping the iPhone HDR look means running strategy (b) with two parallel composites (SDR and HDR) and accepting a synthetic HDR for the background, or exporting SDR Display P3 and dropping the gain map. No source retrieved shows anyone shipping HDR-preserving diffusion composites. iPhone 17 Pro-specific format facts (48 MP HEIC/ProRAW defaults) not retrieved.

## Hypotheses checked

- H1 — Core Image + Core Graphics suffice for composition and overlay without extra models: PARTLY CONFIRMED. For blending, masked grading (F6), scaling and export, Core Image has the primitives, and it has an explicit HDR-aware save path (F20–F22); Core Graphics/CG contexts are the obvious overlay renderer (no source retrieved, judgment). But (i) the subject mask itself comes from a model (Apple's built-in segmentation; 384x512 mask resolution at Apple's camera pipeline, F3), so "no extra model" means "no bundled model"; (ii) depth-based blur needs a depth model (F1, ~50 MB); (iii) learned harmonisation needs a model, though a closed-form colour transfer does not (F9/F10); (iv) blends in a CI graph may return unknown headroom (F21). Verdict on the sentence as written: holds for T0 compositing and overlay, fails for depth effects and learned harmonisation.
- H2 — LUT + depth-blur + sky-replacement ladder gives a visibly "edited" result at a fraction of generative cost: PARTLY CONFIRMED (cost side only). Cost side is supported: masked LUT is one built-in filter (F6), depth is ~34 ms on the ANE at 518x396 on an iPhone 15 Pro Max with a 50 MB model (F1), Apple ships segmentation-driven local adjustments in its own Camera (F3/F4). The perceived-value side ("visibly edited") has no user study or shipped-app evidence retrieved (F8); it is a judgment. Sky replacement realism plausibly needs harmonisation (F7 title only).
- H3 — Colour harmonisation is needed after diffusion to avoid a "pasted" look: PARTLY / PLAUSIBLE, NOT DIRECTLY EVIDENCED. Harmonisation is the literature's standard fix for fg/bg colour mismatch, MKL-Harmonizer humans rated it on par with heavy baselines in AR (F9), and IC-Light exists to harmonise composites by relighting (F13). But no source measures the "pasted look" on diffusion img2img backgrounds, and the other likely causes (hard mask edges, sharpness/grain mismatch, missing contact shadows, lighting direction) are untested here. Colour transfer (F9–F11) covers colour cast, not light direction. Caution from F9: dark objects (a cyclist in dark kit) are the known failure case.
