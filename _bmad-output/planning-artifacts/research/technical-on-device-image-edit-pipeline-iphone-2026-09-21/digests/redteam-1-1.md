## Strongest counter-argument

The conclusion frames memory as a sequencing problem ("load, release, repeat"), but the binding constraint on an iPhone is the per-process jetsam ceiling, which two community Core AI repos put at about 6 GB on a 12 GB device, not 12 GB. It also under-counts the pipeline: an image-to-image restyle with a 4B-class DiT is not three models but at least five components — segmenter, text encoder (for the FLUX.2 klein class, an 8B Qwen3 that alone is multiple GB even at 4-bit), VAE encoder, DiT, VAE decoder — each with its own load/specialise cost. The one publicly documented Core AI conversion of a 4B 4-bit DiT was labelled macOS-only precisely because its ~6.5 GB peak (at 1024x1024) exceeds the iPhone limit; Apple's own iPhone demo of a segmenter-then-second-model chain deliberately used models under 1B parameters and admits large-model first-load specialization is slow; and Apple's own headline iOS 27 image generation/editing moved to Private Cloud Compute. Unloading is not a free reset either: MLX keeps freed buffers unless you cap and clear the cache, and Core ML has a record of retaining IOSurfaces and large malloc blocks after prediction. Finally, every latency number for this model class in the evidence is M4 Max (17-43 s at 1024x1024), not A19 Pro, and none includes cold specialization — so "tens of seconds per photo without jetsam or throttling" is an extrapolation from a Mac benchmark to a phone, whose nearest direct tests are either negative (macOS-only declaration) or anecdotally crashing (Draw Things report). The conclusion may be feasible at 512x512 with careful engineering, but it is currently an untested hypothesis, not an established capability.

## Verdict

**Does not survive as stated; survives only as a plausible-but-unproven engineering hypothesis.** Revisions needed:
- Enumerate every component (text encoder, VAE encoder/decoder included) and give a per-stage peak-memory budget against a stated ceiling (~6.1 GB per community sources; state whether the increased-memory-limit entitlement is assumed and what it yields — I found no measured value for iPhone 17 Pro / iOS 27).
- Separate 512x512 from 1024x1024: the ~6.5 GB peak in item 1 is at 1024x1024 with the text encoder resident. 512x512 cuts activation memory, and — my own inference, not evidence — a FIXED restyle prompt lets you precompute and ship the text embeddings so the 8B encoder never loads on device. That is the most credible rescue and should be tested, not assumed.
- Split latency into warm and first-run (specialization/compile). Apple says first-load is long for very large models; I found no iPhone number for a 4B DiT. My own back-of-envelope from Draw Things' 17 Pro data (FLUX.1 12B, 768x768, ~10 s/step) scaled by parameters and pixels suggests a few seconds per step at 512x512 for 4B — so "tens of seconds warm" is plausible IF the model fits; this is inference and it favours the conclusion.
- Drop "without thermal throttling making it unusable" as a claim unless there is back-to-back run data on a 17 Pro; the only support is a vendor statement.
- Acknowledge the platform-owner signal: Apple sent its high-quality image generation to PCC in iOS 27.
- Prefer the claim "one photo at a time" and "explicit cache clear between stages" over "memory returns to baseline", which no evidence I found demonstrates for Core AI.
